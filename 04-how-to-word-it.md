# How to word it

Word the disclosure in plain, honest language that states clearly that you earn a commission when the audience buys through your links. Avoid vague hints, jargon, and euphemisms; the audience should understand the connection from the words alone.

## Say what it is

Effective wording states the connection directly: that the content contains affiliate links and that you earn a commission when someone buys through them. The audience should not have to interpret or guess. A plain statement of the financial relationship communicates exactly what the rule wants communicated. Directness is the whole job of the wording.

## Avoid vague hints

Vague phrasing fails because it does not actually convey the connection. Hinting at a relationship, or using an obscure term that the audience will not recognize as meaning you are paid, leaves people uninformed. The disclosure must make the material connection understandable, so wording that obscures rather than reveals does not satisfy the requirement. If the audience could read the disclosure and still not realize you earn money, the wording is too vague.

## Keep it brief and clear

A good disclosure is short and unambiguous. It does not need to be long or formal; it needs to be clear. One plain sentence that names the affiliate relationship and the commission does more than a paragraph of legal language that people skip. Brevity helps the disclosure get read, and clarity ensures it is understood once read. Short and plain beats long and dense.

## Match the disclosure to the truth

The wording should accurately reflect the actual relationship. If you earn a commission, say so; if you also received the product free, that too is a material connection worth disclosing. The disclosure should not understate or dress up the relationship. Honest wording that matches the real arrangement is both what the rule requires and what keeps the disclosure trustworthy to the audience. Accuracy in the wording is part of disclosing properly.
