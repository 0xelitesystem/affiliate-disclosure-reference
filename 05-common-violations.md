# Common violations

The common affiliate disclosure violations are no disclosure at all, a disclosure buried where no one sees it, vague or coded wording, a disclosure placed after the link instead of before, and disclosing in one place but not in every context where links appear.

## No disclosure

The clearest violation is simply not disclosing an affiliate relationship. Recommending products you earn from without telling the audience both breaks the rule and the trust. This is the failure the whole framework exists to prevent. Whatever else you get right, the absence of any disclosure where a material connection exists is the core violation.

## Buried disclosure

A disclosure that exists but is hidden, in a footer far from the recommendation, behind a link the audience will not click, or in tiny text, fails the clear-and-conspicuous standard. Present but invisible is close to absent. The violation here is treating disclosure as a box to check rather than a message to deliver, placing it where it technically exists but practically does not reach anyone.

## Vague or coded wording

Disclosures that use vague hints, obscure terms, or coded language that the audience will not understand as meaning you are paid fail the plain-language standard. If a reasonable person could read the disclosure and not grasp that you earn a commission, it has not disclosed anything. The violation is communicating in a way that satisfies a form but not the audience's actual understanding.

## Wrong placement or coverage

Placing the disclosure after the audience has already acted, or disclosing in one piece of content while leaving other content with affiliate links undisclosed, both fail the closeness requirement. The disclosure has to reach the audience before they act and has to appear in every context where recommendations are made. A disclosure that is too late, or that does not cover all the places links appear, leaves real recommendations undisclosed despite good intentions elsewhere.
